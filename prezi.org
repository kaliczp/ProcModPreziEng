  - aids in selecting the model equations or software

* Analytical Modelling

** Subsurface Water Movement


*** 
Example of the governing equation for subsurface water movement. It is the basic equation of MODFLOW.

\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ K_{xx} \frac{\partial h}{\partial x} \right] + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left[ K_{yy} \frac{\partial h}{\partial y} \right] + \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left[ K_{zz} \frac{\partial h}{\partial z} \right] + W = S_{S} \frac{\partial h}{\partial t} \text{,}
\end{equation*}

*** 
    :PROPERTIES:
    :BEAMER_env: block
    :END:
where
#+ATTR_BEAMER: :overlay <+->
- $K_{xx}$, $K_{yy}$, $K_{zz}$ are the hydraulic conductivities
- in the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions,
- $h$ is the piezometric pressure level,
- $W$ is the volume of water inflow and outflow in the given volume,
- $S_s$ is the specific storage,
- $t$ is time.

** Analytical Solution to Subsurface Water Movement

More complex differential equations can only be solved analytically after simplifications.

*** 
#+BEAMER: \onslide*<1>{
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ K_{xx} \frac{\partial h}{\partial x} \right] + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left[ K_{yy} \frac{\partial h}{\partial y} \right] + \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left[ K_{zz} \frac{\partial h}{\partial z} \right] = S_{S} \frac{\partial h}{\partial t} \text{,}
\end{equation*}
considering the whole system as a watershed.
#+BEAMER: }\onslide*<2>{
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ K_{xx} \frac{\partial h}{\partial x} \right] + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left[ K_{yy} \frac{\partial h}{\partial y} \right] + \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left[ K_{zz} \frac{\partial h}{\partial z} \right] = 0 \text{,}
\end{equation*}
assuming there are no changes over time.
#+BEAMER: }\onslide*<3>{
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial z^2} = 0 \text{,}
\end{equation*}
assuming the medium is isotropic.
#+BEAMER: }

** Laplace's Equation
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial z^2} = 0 \text{,}
\end{equation*}
#+BEAMER: \onslide*<2->{
\begin{equation*}
\nabla^2 h = 0
\end{equation*}
#+BEAMER: }\onslide*<3->{
\begin{equation*}
\Delta h = 0
\end{equation*}
#+BEAMER: }


** Closed Mirror Horizontal Drain
*** 

Let's try to solve the previous equation in the horizontal drain!
#+BEAMER: \onslide*<2->{
The entire aquifer is under pressure, so in a homogeneous medium,
$\frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial z^2}=0$. 
#+BEAMER: }\onslide*<3->{
Assuming the gallery is sufficiently long, $\frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial y^2}=0$.
#+BEAMER: }

*** 

#+BEAMER: \alt<4>{
$$\frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial x^2} = 0$$
#+BEAMER: }{

** Jósági mutatószámok

Nash-Sutcliffe hatékonysági mutató:
\begin{equation*}
NS = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(O_i-M_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n(O_i-\bar{O})^2},
\end{equation*}


RMSE (root-mean-square error -- a négyzetes középhiba gyöke):

\begin{equation*}
RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(O_i-M_i)^2}
\end{equation*}


ahol $O$ megfigyelés (observation), $M$ modell output.
